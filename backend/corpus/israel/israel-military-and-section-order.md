# Military, informal experience and section order on an Israeli-market CV

**Split from `israel-personal-details` on 2026-08-28, on trust level.** Metadata is per document, so
a single file cannot hold both institutional and practitioner-derived rules without the weaker ones
inheriting the stronger tag.

Every rule here traces to **S-002** — the Israeli Ministry of Defence *Hachvana* career guidance for
discharged soldiers, which the source register calls the highest-value Israel-specific source found
and the clearest case where Israel-first is justified by demonstrated difference rather than by
origin. No global source in the register covers this ground at all.

The section-order rule's *experienced-candidate* branch is global convention rather than an Israeli
claim, so it needs no Israeli sourcing and does not dilute the institutional tag. **It is
deliberately not split into two rules**: the condition is what makes the instruction correct, and
FR-037 requires a rule to carry its own conditions.

## Rules

- Where the candidate's profile records military service AND the service is relevant to this
  posting, present it as capability rather than as rank or unit title — what was managed, decided,
  coordinated or maintained, and at what scale. Where the profile records service but nothing in it
  bears on the posting, keep it as a brief factual line and do not expand it. Never infer duties,
  responsibilities or skills from a rank, a unit name or a service period that the profile does not
  state: the reframing is a change of emphasis, not a source of new facts.

- For a candidate with professional work experience, place the experience section above education.
  For a candidate without professional experience — a recent graduate, or someone whose record is
  military and volunteer work — place education first and treat military, volunteer and
  youth-movement roles as experience sections in their own right rather than as footnotes.

- Where the profile records what the candidate actually did during service, express it in the
  vocabulary of work: operating under time pressure, deciding with incomplete information,
  coordinating people and equipment, training others, maintaining systems. Use only the activities
  the profile records; the translation is one of vocabulary, and the underlying facts stay whatever
  the candidate wrote.

- Where the candidate has informal experience the profile records — freelance work, a family
  business, unpaid work for a community organisation — give it a section of its own rather than
  omitting it or folding it into a list of interests. For a candidate whose professional record is
  thin, this is frequently the strongest evidence available.

- Where volunteering, a youth-movement role or a training course answers something this posting
  asks for, connect it explicitly to that requirement rather than listing it as an activity. Where
  it answers nothing the posting asks for, it is a candidate for dropping like any other item.
