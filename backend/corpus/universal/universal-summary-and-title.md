# The summary and the professional title

Derived from the CareerHQ house rubric (S-018), so `trust_level: internal`. The summary is the most
frequently rewritten block in a tailored resume and the easiest place to drift from the profile,
because it is prose rather than a list of facts — which is why several rules here bound it rather
than encourage it.

## Rules

- The summary states what the person is and what they have done, in the terms this posting uses. It
  is not a statement of ambition, of what they are looking for, or of what they hope to grow into:
  a reader is deciding whether to interview the candidate for this role, not learning their plans.

- Every claim in the summary must be supported by something further down the resume. The summary is
  a reading of the document, not an addition to it, so a skill or a domain that appears nowhere in
  the experience, projects or skills sections does not belong in the opening paragraph either.

- Where the posting names a professional title the candidate's record genuinely supports, use the
  posting's wording for the professional title that heads the resume — the candidate's own
  description of what they are, not the job title recorded against any employer. Where the record
  does not support it — a different seniority, a different discipline — keep the candidate's own
  title unchanged rather than adopting the posting's. A title recorded against a role is an
  employment fact and is never changed to match a posting.

- Keep the summary to a few sentences of substance rather than a paragraph of qualities. Adjectives
  that any candidate could claim — passionate, detail-oriented, results-driven, hard-working — take
  space from the specifics that distinguish this candidate and tell a reader nothing they can check.

- Lead the summary with the candidate's strongest genuine match to this posting, not with years of
  experience. Where the years are themselves the strongest match — a role asking for depth the
  candidate demonstrably has — lead with them; otherwise they are a weaker opening than the work.

- Where the candidate has no summary in their profile, do not invent one from the rest of the
  record. Composing a summary is writing new claims in the candidate's voice about their own
  career, and that is the owner's decision to make rather than the agent's to make for them.
