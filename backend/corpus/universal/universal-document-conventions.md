# Document conventions

**`trust_level: internal`, `origin_source_ids` deliberately empty — and this file exists because
both of those had to be true.** Its two rules were authored inside
`universal-structure-and-ordering`, which is tagged `institutional` on S-001's authority. S-001
contributes CV format choice, the functional-CV warning, volunteer and youth-movement inclusion,
listing separate roles at one employer, and a 10-15 year window. **It contributes neither of these
rules**, so both were inheriting institutional standing they had not earned.

Metadata is per document, so the only way to stop that inheritance is a separate file. Moved
verbatim on 2026-08-28 — no rule text changed, no rule added.

These are CareerHQ product judgement, labelled as such. That is a weaker claim than S-001's, and
the weaker claim is the true one.

**The section-order rule carries its own exception (2026-08-28, finding F4).** Two other rules
instruct a section reordering — the Israeli-market rule on experience-versus-education, and the
early-career rule on placing a strong project above weaker professional work. One-rule-one-chunk
means none of the three can reference the others, and retrieval can return this prohibition alone.
Without the exception stated *here*, this rule contradicts both whenever it arrives by itself.

The escape is deliberately narrow: it turns on **the record**, not on the posting. Reordering
sections to suit a particular employer remains exactly what this rule forbids.

## Rules

- Keep section order stable and conventional so a reader can find what they expect without hunting.
  Reordering the items *within* a section by relevance is tailoring; reordering the sections
  themselves is redesigning the document, and it costs the reader more than it gains. Where a
  candidate's record makes the conventional order actively misleading — a candidate whose strongest
  evidence is not professional work — the order follows the evidence rather than the convention;
  that is a property of the record, not a tailoring decision made per posting.

- Give every role an employer, a title and a date range as the profile records them. A gap the
  profile shows is the candidate's to explain and may not be closed by widening an adjacent date
  range or omitting the role that ended.
