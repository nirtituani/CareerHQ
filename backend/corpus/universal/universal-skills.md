# The skills section

Derived from the house rubric (S-018). The skills section is the one place where adding a single
word fabricates a claim, so most of these rules govern what may not appear rather than what should.

## Rules

- Order skills by relevance to this posting rather than alphabetically, by category convention, or
  by the order the profile stores them. A reader checks the first few against the posting's
  requirements and rarely reads to the end of a long list.

- Do not add a skill the profile does not list. A tool named only in a job description the candidate
  read, a technology adjacent to one they used, or a skill implied by a role title are all additions
  rather than reorderings, and the candidate will be asked about whatever appears here.

- Where the profile lists a skill under a name the posting does not use, and the two demonstrably
  refer to the same thing, prefer the posting's name. Where they refer to related but distinct
  things — a language and a framework, a protocol and a product — keep both names as the profile
  has them rather than collapsing one into the other.

- Group skills only where the profile already supports the grouping, and keep the grouping stable
  across a resume. Inventing a category such as "Cloud & DevOps" to hold two unrelated entries makes
  a thin list look deliberate, which is a presentation of depth the record does not support.

- Remove skills that serve no requirement in this posting rather than keeping them for completeness.
  A long list dilutes the relevant entries, and a reader who scans it is looking for the posting's
  terms rather than an inventory of everything the candidate has touched.

- Do not attach a proficiency rating, a percentage, a bar or a number of years to a skill unless the
  profile records that value. A self-assessed level the candidate never stated is a fabricated
  claim in the most checkable part of the document.
