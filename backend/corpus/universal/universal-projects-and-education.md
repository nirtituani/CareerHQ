# Projects, education and certifications

**`origin_source_ids` is empty, and was `[S-018]` until 2026-08-28.** The house rubric has twelve
rules and **none of them concerns projects, education or certifications** — the citation was
decorative, which makes an unsourced rule look sourced and makes the field useless as the thing a
reader checks. These are CareerHQ product judgement, and `trust_level: internal` already says so.

These sections carry the most fixed facts in a resume —
degrees, dates, institutions, credentials — so tailoring them is mostly selection and ordering
rather than rewriting.

## Rules

- Do not alter a degree name, an institution, a credential title or a date. These are checkable
  facts and a reader may verify them; rewording a qualification to sound closer to the posting
  misrepresents a record that an employer can confirm independently.

- Where the candidate has several projects, keep the ones that serve a requirement in this posting
  and drop the rest rather than shortening all of them. Where a project serves no requirement but is
  the only evidence of a skill the posting asks for, keep it and lead with that skill.

- Describe a project by what the candidate built and what it does, not by the technologies alone. A
  list of technologies is already in the skills section, and a reader learns nothing about judgement
  or scope from seeing it repeated.

- Where a certification has an expiry or a date the profile records, keep it as recorded. An expired
  credential presented without its date reads as current, which is a misrepresentation the candidate
  did not choose to make.

- Include coursework, academic projects or a thesis only where the candidate's professional record
  does not already demonstrate the same skill. For a candidate with relevant work experience these
  displace stronger evidence; for one without, they may be the strongest evidence available.

- Do not infer a graduation date, a grade, or a field of study from other facts in the profile. A
  degree with no recorded date is written without one rather than with a date derived from the
  candidate's employment history.
