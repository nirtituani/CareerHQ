# Writing an experience bullet

`trust_level: internal` because these derive from the shipped CareerHQ house rubric (S-018) rather
than from an outside authority.

**S-001 was cited here and was removed on 2026-08-28.** S-001 (Israel Employment Service)
contributes CV format choice, volunteer and youth-movement inclusion, listing separate roles at one
employer, and a 10–15-year window. **None of the five rules below derives from any of that** — every
one traces to S-018. The citation was decorative, and a decorative citation is worse than none: it
makes an unsourced rule look sourced, and it makes `origin_source_ids` useless as the thing a
reader checks. Two of them **extend** a rubric rule with a condition the rubric
did not carry; the rest restate it. Nothing here conflicts with the rubric — where a conflict
appears in a later file, it must be stated in that file rather than resolved silently.

## Rules

- Open each bullet with a concrete verb naming what the person did — built, led, migrated, reduced,
  owned — rather than "responsible for" or "worked on". Both of those describe a job description
  rather than a person's work, and neither says whether the thing was accomplished.

- Keep one idea to a bullet. Where a bullet carries two, split it only if the profile supports both
  independently; where it carries two and only one is relevant to this posting, cut to the relevant
  one rather than compressing both into a denser line.

- State scale wherever the profile records it — team size, request volume, data volume, budget,
  duration. Scale is what separates two candidates whose bullets otherwise read identically. Where
  the profile records no scale for an item, write the item without scale; do not signal its absence
  with vague intensifiers such as "large-scale" or "high-volume".

- Order the bullets within each role by relevance to this posting rather than by chronology or by
  the order the profile happens to store them. A reader moves top-down through a role and stops
  early, so the most relevant work must not sit fourth.

## Removed, and why it must not come back

A fifth rule was drafted and **removed on 2026-08-28**: *"drop an item that serves no requirement in
this posting rather than shortening it."*

It duplicated `tailoring-relevance-and-selection`'s drop-versus-compress rule almost exactly — both
derive from the same house-rubric rule, and retrieval could return both and spend the FR-014 budget
twice on one idea. **Selection is the tailoring file's subject; writing a bullet is this file's**,
so the surviving copy lives there.

Its one distinct clause — that dropping is scoped to this application and never edits the master
profile — is not lost: `integrity-owner-authority` states it as a standing obligation, which is a
stronger home for it than a bullet-writing rule.
