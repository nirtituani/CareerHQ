# Senior, staff and principal candidates

Derived from S-009 (career-ladders.dev). At these levels the differentiator documented across career
frameworks is scope and effect on others rather than personal output, and a resume that reads like
a strong mid-level one under-sells the candidate.

## Rules

- Lead senior roles with scope and consequence — what the candidate was responsible for, what
  changed because of it — rather than with the technologies used. Technology choice is expected at
  this level and rarely distinguishes two senior candidates.

- Where the profile records influence on people or on how work was done — mentoring, setting a
  standard, changing a process, leading a migration — keep it, because that is what separates senior
  from mid-level in most career frameworks. Where the profile records none of it, do not supply it.

- Compress early roles that predate the candidate's current level into brief entries and give the
  space to recent work, unless an early role is directly relevant to this posting. A senior reader
  is evaluating the last few years most closely.

- Where the candidate's title and their actual scope disagree in the profile, present the title as
  recorded and let the scope show in the content. Retitling a role upward to match a posting
  misrepresents a checkable fact, and the scope makes the case without it.
