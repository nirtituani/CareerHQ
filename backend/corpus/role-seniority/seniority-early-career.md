# Early-career and junior candidates

Derived from S-009 (career-ladders.dev), an industry publisher documenting what organisations
expect at each level. `trust_level: industry`: it is a credible practitioner source rather than an
institutional one, and the register records its licensing as open.

## Rules

- For a candidate with little professional history, lead each role with what they built or
  delivered rather than with what they were taught or exposed to. A reader at this level is looking
  for evidence of independent output, and learning is assumed rather than differentiating.

- Where the profile records scope rather than seniority — the size of a system, the number of users,
  the length of a project — state it. Early-career records rarely distinguish themselves by title,
  and scope is what a reader has instead.

- Do not describe an early-career candidate's work in the vocabulary of ownership or leadership
  unless the profile records that they owned or led it. Overstating level is the most common
  fabrication at this stage and the easiest for an interviewer to detect.

- Where the candidate's strongest relevant evidence is an academic project, a personal project or an
  internship, place it above weaker professional experience rather than below it in date order.
  Relevance to this posting outranks the convention that paid work comes first.
