# What tailoring may never do

**Authored, never sourced.** Integrity rules are CareerHQ product obligations under Constitution
Principle III and AI-008, not advice gathered from anyone — so `trust_level` is `internal` for
every rule in this file, and citing an outside source for one would misrepresent where the
obligation comes from.

These are also the rules that must survive retrieval. They are pinned rather than ranked: a run
whose posting happens to be semantically distant from the word "honesty" is exactly the run that
needs them.

## Rules

- Never introduce a number the profile does not already contain. Team sizes, percentages, latencies,
  revenue, user counts and durations may be restated, moved or emphasised, but never estimated,
  rounded up, inferred from context, or supplied as a plausible range. A profile that gives no
  figure is to be written without one.

- Never claim experience with a technology, tool, methodology or domain that the profile does not
  record. Mirroring the posting's vocabulary is permitted only where the profile already supports
  the claim under a different name — "Postgres" may be written as "PostgreSQL"; "worked alongside
  the ML team" may not be written as "machine learning experience".

- Do not treat keyword coverage as a target to be met. There is no proportion of the posting's
  terms that a resume is supposed to contain, and pursuing one produces claims the profile cannot
  support. Relevance to what the candidate actually did governs; the overlap with the posting is a
  consequence of that, never the objective.

- Where the profile shows adjacent rather than direct experience, describe it as what it was.
  Presenting adjacent work as direct is the same fabrication as inventing it outright, and is
  harder for the candidate to notice before they are asked about it in an interview.

- Never remove a qualifying word that the candidate wrote — "assisted with", "contributed to",
  "supported" — in order to strengthen a claim. Those words are the candidate's own account of
  their role, and deleting one converts their statement into a different and larger one.
