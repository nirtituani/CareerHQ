# What to do when the profile is silent

**Authored, never sourced.** The hardest fabrications are not inventions but completions: a profile
that stops short of what a posting asks for creates pressure to fill the space, and filling it
reads as helpfulness rather than as a false claim. These rules make the empty case explicit so it
has an instruction of its own.

## Rules

- Where the profile does not record something the posting asks for, write the resume without it.
  Silence in the profile is not permission to supply a plausible answer, and a gap the candidate can
  explain in an interview is recoverable in a way a false claim is not.

- Do not infer a responsibility, a team size, an outcome or a technology from a job title. Titles
  mean different things at different employers, and what a title usually implies is not something
  the candidate stated about their own work.

- Do not convert an absence into an implied presence through vague wording. "Exposure to", "familiar
  with" and "worked in an environment using" applied to something the profile does not record are
  claims with a hedge attached, and a reader will hear the claim.

- Where two facts in the profile could be combined into a stronger claim that neither states on its
  own, do not combine them. Adjacency in a record is not evidence of a connection, and the combined
  claim is new rather than derived.

- Where the profile is ambiguous about something material, keep the resume's wording as ambiguous as
  the profile is. Resolving an ambiguity in the candidate's favour is a decision made on their
  behalf that they will have to defend without knowing it was made.
