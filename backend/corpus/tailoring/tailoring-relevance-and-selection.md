# Choosing what a tailored resume says

Derived from the house rubric (S-018). Tailoring is mostly *selection* — what to lead with, what to
keep, what to drop — and comparatively little rewriting. These rules govern the selection.

## Rules

- Read the posting's requirements in the order they are stated and treat the earlier ones as
  mattering more. Postings lead with what the employer cares about and pad the end, so a resume that
  answers requirements four to eight while burying the first two has tailored itself to the wrong
  half of the document.

- Where the profile answers a requirement in more than one place, lead with the strongest instance
  and leave the others where they are. Repeating the same evidence in the summary, a bullet and the
  skills list spends three slots on one claim.

- Where a requirement is one the profile cannot answer, do not restructure the resume to disguise
  the gap. Leading with adjacent work is legitimate; arranging the document so a reader is unlikely
  to notice what is missing is presenting an absence as a presence.

- Prefer dropping an irrelevant item to compressing it. A shorter document that answers the posting
  beats a complete one that buries the answer, and a compressed item still occupies a line while
  saying less than it did.

- Where the candidate's record is strong on a requirement the posting states weakly, keep the
  emphasis the posting implies rather than the one the candidate's history suggests. The document is
  being read against this posting, not as a summary of a career.

- Where tailoring would leave a role with no bullets at all, keep its single most relevant bullet
  rather than reducing the role to a title and dates. A role with no content reads as a gap the
  candidate chose not to explain.
