# Mirroring the posting's vocabulary

Derived from the house rubric (S-018). Mirroring is adopted; **quota-chasing is refused** — the
research digests carried a keyword-coverage proportion and it is a direct route to claims the
profile cannot support. The integrity file states that refusal as an obligation; this file states
the practice that replaces it.

## Rules

- Where the profile and the posting name the same thing differently, prefer the posting's word. A
  reader matching a resume against a requirement they wrote is looking for their own vocabulary, and
  a synonym costs a match that the candidate's record actually earns.

- Mirror a term only where the profile already supports the claim under another name. A term the
  profile cannot back is a fabricated claim regardless of how prominently the posting features it,
  and mirroring is a translation of the record rather than an extension of it.

- Where the posting uses a term ambiguously and the profile uses a precise one, keep the precise
  term. Adopting a vaguer word to match makes the claim harder for a reader to evaluate and gains
  nothing that the precise term did not already convey.

- Where a posting's phrasing is an internal or company-specific label with no external meaning, do
  not adopt it. The resume may be read by people outside that team, and a borrowed internal label
  says less about the candidate than the words they used themselves.

- Do not repeat a mirrored term beyond where it belongs. Placing a requirement's vocabulary in every
  section to increase its density produces a document that reads as written for a machine, and a
  human reader is the one who decides on an interview.
