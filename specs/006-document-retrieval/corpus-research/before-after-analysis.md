# Before/After CV Analysis

## Purpose

This document records observations extracted from six Before → After CV
examples.

The examples are being evaluated as potential **demonstrative knowledge**
for the CareerHQ resume-tailoring knowledge corpus.

Important distinction:

- These observations are derived from the examples.
- They are **candidate patterns**, not validated universal CV rules.
- A pattern should only become an authoritative guideline after it is
  supported by additional evidence such as trusted external sources,
  multiple examples, or explicit product decisions.
- The Before → After examples themselves should remain separate from
  normative guidelines.

The current product target is:

- English-language CVs.
- Primarily the Israeli technology job market.
- Tailoring should be role-aware and seniority-aware.
- Candidate facts must remain grounded in the Professional Profile;
  examples must never be treated as permission to invent facts.

# 1. Observed Recurring Patterns

## 1.1 Role Positioning

### Observation

The improved CV tends to establish a clearer professional identity aligned
with the target role.

Examples include moving from broad positioning toward a more specific
professional identity such as Backend Developer, Senior Data & Business
Analytics Project Manager, or a technical customer-facing role.

### Candidate guideline

> When tailoring a CV, make the candidate's professional positioning
> clearly relevant to the target role, using only roles, experience,
> technologies, and capabilities supported by the candidate's source
> material.

### Evidence

- Example 1 — Senior Android / Mobile
- Example 2 — Technical customer-facing / Presales
- Example 5 — Backend / Blockchain
- Example 6 — Data / Analytics Project Management

### Strength

**Strong observation.**

The pattern appears across substantially different role families.

### Caution

This does not imply that every CV should be narrowly positioned or that
relevant alternative experience should be removed.

compressed rather than necessarily being deleted.

## 1.3 Achievements Over Generic Responsibilities

### Observation

Several improved CVs move from responsibility-oriented descriptions toward
outcomes, achievements, or concrete impact.

Examples frequently change a pattern similar to:

> Responsible for / managed / worked on X

toward:

> Did X resulting in Y.

### Candidate guideline

> When supported by the candidate's source material, prefer concrete
> achievements and outcomes over generic descriptions of responsibilities.

### Evidence

- Example 1
- Example 2
- Example 4
- Example 6

### Strength

**Strong observation.**

### Caution

This does not mean that responsibilities should always be removed.
Responsibilities can establish scope and may be particularly important when
measurable outcomes are unavailable.

## 1.4 Quantified Impact

### Observation

The improved CVs frequently surface quantitative evidence such as:

- revenue
- savings
- users
- scale
- percentage improvements
- number of systems
- number of teams
- budget size
- operational scale

### Candidate guideline

> When credible quantitative evidence exists in the candidate's source
> material, surface it where it strengthens the description of impact,
> scope, or scale.

### Evidence

- Example 1
- Example 2
- Example 4
- Example 6

### Strength

**Strong observation.**

### Critical integrity constraint

Numbers must come from the candidate's source material.

The examples must **not** teach the model that adding a large number makes
a CV better, or give the model permission to invent metrics.

### Rejected interpretation

> "Always add metrics."

This is **not supported**.

The supported observation is:

> Use existing, credible quantitative evidence when it is relevant.

## 1.5 Technical Specificity

### Observation

For technical or technically oriented roles, improved CVs tend to make
relevant technologies, systems, technical domains, and scale more explicit.

Examples include technologies, platforms, architectures, data systems,
backend infrastructure, mobile technologies, and technical scope.

### Candidate guideline

> For technical roles, make relevant technical experience and capabilities
> explicit when they are supported by the candidate's source material.

### Evidence

- Example 1
- Example 2
- Example 5
- Example 6

### Strength

**Strong observation for technical roles.**

### Caution

This is role-dependent. It should not become a rule that every CV should
contain more technologies.

## 1.6 Action-Oriented Language

### Observation

Improved bullets frequently replace generic or passive responsibility
language with more direct descriptions of what the candidate actually did.

### Candidate guideline

> Prefer specific, action-oriented descriptions of the candidate's actual
> contributions over generic responsibility phrasing.

### Evidence

- Examples 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6

### Strength

**Strong observation.**

### Caution

Action verbs alone do not create a strong bullet. They should be combined
with meaningful scope, context, technical detail, or outcome where supported.

## 1.8 Focused Summary

### Observation

The improved CVs generally use the Summary to establish:

1. professional identity,
2. relevant experience/scope,
3. relevant capabilities,
4. and, where available, meaningful impact.

The Summary becomes less focused on generic personal attributes.

### Candidate guideline

> Use the Summary to establish the candidate's professional identity and
> most relevant value proposition quickly, rather than spending most of
> the space on generic personal attributes.

### Observation

Example 5 particularly demonstrates a shift from broad technology/skill
lists toward demonstrating technical capabilities through actual work and

### Candidate guideline

> When possible, demonstrate relevant skills through concrete experience,
> projects, and achievements rather than relying solely on a standalone
> skills list.

### Evidence

- Example 5
- Also related to Examples 1 and 6

### Strength

**Moderate observation.**

### Caution

## 1.11 Career / Experience Positioning

### Observation

The examples show that tailoring is not limited to rewriting individual
sentences. It can involve deciding which parts of an existing career
should receive prominence for a target role.

Example 5 is particularly clear: broad experience is reframed toward a
Backend / Blockchain identity.

### Candidate guideline

> Tailoring may change the hierarchy and emphasis of existing experience,
> not only the wording of individual bullets.

# 2. Patterns That Must NOT Become Rules

The examples are insufficient evidence for the following universal claims.

## 2.1 Always Add Metrics

Rejected.

Correct interpretation:

> Use existing quantitative evidence when relevant and supported.

---

## 2.2 Always Make the CV One Page

Rejected.

The examples demonstrate compression and prioritization, not a universal
page-count requirement.

---

## 2.3 Always Add More Technologies

Rejected.

Technical specificity is useful when relevant to the target role and
supported by the candidate's experience.

---

## 2.4 Always Remove Non-Relevant Information

Rejected.

Information may be compressed, deprioritized, or retained depending on the
target role and seniority.

---

## 2.5 Always Use the Same CV Structure

Rejected.

The examples demonstrate principles rather than one universal template.

---

## 2.6 Always Rewrite Every Bullet

Rejected.

Tailoring can involve selection, prioritization, ordering, compression,
or leaving already-strong content unchanged.

---

# 3. Demonstrative Knowledge vs Normative Knowledge

The Before → After examples should be represented separately from
normative guidelines.

## Normative knowledge

Examples:

> Prefer measurable outcomes when supported by the candidate's source
> material.

> Prioritize role-relevant experience.

> Avoid unsupported claims.

These are candidate guidelines that can potentially become corpus rules.

## Demonstrative knowledge

Examples:

```text
Before:
Responsible for managing...

After:
[Specific achievement-oriented version]

Transformation:
responsibility → outcome

Applicable patterns:
- achievement focus
- quantified impact
- action-oriented language
